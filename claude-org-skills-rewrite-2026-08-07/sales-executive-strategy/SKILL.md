# Sales Executive Strategy

## SOURCES OF TRUTH — read before coaching

| Need | Go to |
|---|---|
| The actual deal — stage, amount, history | Ask `chief-hubspot-officer`; never quote deal data from memory |
| District, contact, and relationship context | G-Brain (`Bookmarked GBrain` connector) |
| Any language that reaches a customer | Confluence **174981121** — Brand + Design Agent Operating Rules |
| Regulatory questions | `compliance-advisor` skill |
| Current targets and priorities | Confluence **187760767** — BSH-2026 Strategic Home Sprint |

**Do not carry milestone dates or revenue targets in your head from this file.** They move. Pull them from the sprint page. This skill carries method, not numbers.

## DEFAULT POSTURE

Tactical and coaching-oriented. Help the person win the specific conversation in front of them. Ground every piece of coaching in the actual deal record — pull HubSpot data first. Hypothetical coaching produces hypothetical results.

## FRAMEWORK 1: TEACH — TAILOR — TAKE CONTROL

**Teach.** Lead with an insight the customer does not already have about their own situation. Not a product feature — a reframe of their problem. The test: *does this teach them something new?* If not, it is a brochure, not a pitch.

**Tailor.** Pitch to what this specific person is accountable for. A superintendent is accountable for exposure and defensibility. A librarian is accountable for her week and her professional judgment. A board member is accountable for what happens when something escalates. Generic pitches fail because nobody is accountable for "efficiency."

**Take control.** Stalls happen when we cede control of the process. On any stalled deal, ask: who is driving the next step — us or them? Then re-propose a specific next action with a date.

## FRAMEWORK 2: DUNFORD PITCH STRUCTURE

Build the pitch in this order, never skipping to the end:

1. **The insight** — what is true about their world that they have not fully reckoned with.
2. **The alternatives** — what they would do if we did not exist, stated fairly.
3. **The differentiated capability** — what we have that those alternatives do not.
4. **The value that creates** — for *this* buyer, in their terms.
5. **The proof** — evidence, not adjectives.

**Investor story ≠ customer story.** Investors buy the market and the trajectory. Customers buy relief from a problem they have this quarter. Do not mix them.

## FRAMEWORK 3: OBJECTION HANDLING

Handle objections by locating them first — most are one of four things wearing a disguise:

| Real objection | Sounds like |
|---|---|
| No urgency | "Let's revisit next year." |
| No budget path | "We'd need to go out to bid." |
| No belief | "How is this different from what we have?" |
| No authority | "I'll need to run this by some people." |

Answer the real one. Answering the disguise wastes the meeting.

**On competitors:** never disparage. The honest framing is that circulation systems and intelligence systems do different jobs, and a district needs both. That framing wins because it is true and it does not force the buyer to admit a past mistake.

## FRAMEWORK 4: PRE-CALL PREP

1. Pull the deal record and the last touch.
2. Pull the person and district from G-Brain — what do we already know about them?
3. Identify the one insight to teach in this specific call.
4. Decide the specific next step you will propose, with a date.
5. Name the most likely objection and your response.

## LANGUAGE DISCIPLINE

Anything reaching a customer follows canon (174981121). Load it before drafting outreach. In particular: no "banned books" framing, no unqualified "AI-powered," no multi-state claims, no specific cost or time figures that canon lists under Never-say, and never framing that positions Bookmarked as replacing librarians.

## WHAT THIS SKILL DOES NOT DO

- Forecast, or assess pipeline health as a system.
- Decide territory, comp, or team structure.
- Quote deal or ARR figures directly — that is `chief-hubspot-officer`.
