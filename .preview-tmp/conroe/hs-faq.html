The basics

What Bookmarked is, and what OnShelf does.

What is Bookmarked?

Bookmarked is the Book Intelligence Company. We believe libraries should be the most vibrant rooms in a school, and that librarians should be matching books with readers, not managing spreadsheets. We give districts a faster, more confident way to review and manage their collections so the right book can reach the right reader.

What is OnShelf?

OnShelf is the platform districts log into. It is powered by Book Intelligence, and it brings research, review, and the book-order approval process — what we call Acquisition Review — into one place, so your team spends less time on paperwork and more time with students.

What is Book Intelligence?

Book Intelligence gathers what is publicly known about a book and organizes it for decision-making: audience and age guidance, content ratings, suitability, advisory notes, and any record of the title being challenged or restricted elsewhere. Every claim carries its source and the date we captured it. It draws only on public sources about a book. It does not read or analyze the contents of a book.

Is this a library management system?

No. Legacy systems list books. Bookmarked understands books. Your library management system keeps doing what it does best. OnShelf sits alongside it to handle the research, review, and compliance work.

Your judgment, your collection

Every call is yours to make.

Does Bookmarked decide what books we can have?

No. We provide intelligence, not instructions. Data, not directives. OnShelf surfaces information, and your librarians and your district make every call, guided by your own policies and community values. Bookmarked informs. Districts decide.

Does OnShelf remove or ban books?

No. OnShelf never removes, restricts, or reclassifies anything on its own. Across the districts we work with, when a title needs action it is far more often reviewed and reclassified than pulled, and the collection stays intact. We exist to help keep books on shelves, not take them off.

Does Bookmarked replace librarians?

Never. No software can replace the professional judgment a librarian builds over a career, and we are not trying to. OnShelf handles the time-consuming research and paperwork so librarians get their time, and their mission, back.

Where does your information come from, and is it biased?

Book Intelligence aggregates publicly available information about a book from across the spectrum. What makes it trustworthy is not which sources we favor — it is that you can check every claim yourself.

Four things make that real. Every claim carries its source and the date we captured it. We write what the record actually says — "appeared on a 2025 challenge list in another district" — never "this book is banned," because the first statement stays true and the second one does not. We verify by reading the page a claim comes from rather than trusting the website it sits on, so a catalog listing or a search result is never treated as evidence that a title was challenged. And claims have a life cycle: active, historical, or retracted. We never erase history, and we always remove what turns out to be false.

The goal is not to tell you what to think. It is to give you the full, consistent picture, with the receipts, so you can decide with confidence. That is what lets us serve districts across the political spectrum.

Who can see the flags and review information?

Your district decides who on your team has access, and the flags stay inside your team. Librarians and reviewers see the flags and the review detail. Parents never do.

There is exactly one parent-facing surface, and it exists because the law requires it: when your team submits a book order for the 30-day public comment period SB 13 requires, OnShelf publishes that list of titles to a public page. Parents see the list of titles under consideration. They do not see our flags, and they do not see your review notes.

Compliance

Paperwork becomes a documented process.

How does OnShelf help with Texas requirements like SB 13?

SB 13 requires that new library material be posted publicly for a 30-day parent review period before board approval. OnShelf runs that process end to end: your librarians build the list, administrators review it with Book Intelligence in view, the list publishes to a public URL for the comment period, and the board sees a clean, reviewed, approved list at the end. Every step is recorded — so when a challenge arrives it moves through a documented process with a defensible system of record, instead of becoming a crisis.

Will using OnShelf mean we end up pulling more books?

The opposite. The worry we hear most is that a compliance tool means fewer books. What we see is that collections stay intact: titles are reviewed and reclassified, not removed. OnShelf is designed to keep books moving to students, with a record you can stand behind.

Accuracy and keeping it current

We would rather tell you than let you assume.

How current is this information?

Challenge and restriction data refreshes on a 21-day cycle today, and we are actively shortening it — the path is 21 days to 14 to 7 to daily as we scale the sources. Book metadata and enrichment refresh when a collection is uploaded and on every pipeline run after that. We would rather tell you the cadence than let you assume it is instant.

What if a flag looks wrong to me?

Tell us. You know your collection better than any system does, and a librarian catching a bad flag is the most valuable feedback we get. Send it to Patrick, or raise it with Mindy in your every-other-week check-in.

Here is what happens next. If a claim turns out to be unsubstantiated, we retract it. If it was true but is no longer current, we mark it historical rather than deleting the record, so the audit trail stays intact. That loop is how the intelligence gets better — and Conroe's librarians are close to the front of it.

Security, integrations, and support

What we hold, what we connect to, and who to call.

Is our student data secure?

Yes — and in Conroe's case the answer starts somewhere better: we do not take in student data at all. Your configuration does not use OnShelf's circulation or restricted-access features, so no student records are sent to us. Book Intelligence, the engine that researches books, works on books rather than on students.

If Conroe ISD later turns on circulation or restricted access, student data would come into scope. Either way, we sign a data privacy agreement with your district and follow industry-standard security controls, and we are glad to walk your technology team through exactly what is and is not stored.

Does OnShelf work with our existing catalog?

Yes, alongside it. Your catalog keeps handling circulation, and OnShelf handles the research, review, and compliance work. Different jobs, different tools, and you get the benefit of both. You bring your collection in with a simple export from your catalog.

Can OnShelf handle a large district?

Yes. OnShelf supports collections from a single campus to the largest districts in Texas, and it is trusted by 150+ Texas school districts.

Who do I contact when I need help?

Patrick Reid is Conroe's Customer Success lead — patrick@bookmarked.com. For anything involving rostering, campus setup, or access, start there. Mindy is your district lead, and we meet every other week.

How do I get my campus set up?

Your campus collections are already loaded. Access comes through your district's rostering, which Mindy is coordinating. If you cannot get in, tell Mindy or Patrick rather than waiting — it is usually a five-minute fix on our end.

How do I join the library advisory group?

Ask Mindy or Patrick. We are always looking for librarian voices, and the advisory group is where workflow changes get shaped before they ship.
