A welcome from the Bookmarked team

Welcome,
Conroe ISD librarians.

Thank you for the work you do. Ahead of Thursday's working session, here is a little about who we are, what we believe, and what to expect. We are honored to partner with your team.

Why we are here

Libraries should be the most vibrant rooms in a school.

And the person who makes that happen is the librarian.

No software will ever replace the judgment you have built over a career, and we are not trying to. You did not become a librarian to spend your days on paperwork. We built OnShelf to take the compliance busywork off your plate, so you can get back to what you do best: connecting students with the books that change their lives.

Born and raised in Texas

Bookmarked was born and raised in Texas. The idea came from a Texas superintendent, and the product has been shaped ever since by Texas librarians, Texas districts, and Texas service centers.

What OnShelf is, in plain language

A research and review partner. Not a decision-maker.

OnShelf is the platform, powered by Book Intelligence. For any title, it pulls together what is publicly known about that book — audience and age guidance, content ratings, suitability, advisory notes, and any record of the title being challenged or restricted elsewhere — and puts it on one screen, with the source and the capture date behind every claim. What used to take a half-dozen browser tabs is organized in one place.

What it is

  • One source of truth your whole team can rely on.
  • A faster review-and-order workflow that replaces the Google forms and spreadsheets.
  • A documented, defensible record for the moment a challenge arrives.

What it is not

  • It does not remove, restrict, or reclassify anything on its own. You decide, every time.
  • It does not judge a book good or bad, or tell you what to order.
  • It does not read or analyze the contents of a book. Everything comes from public sources about the book.

The flags stay inside your team. Librarians and reviewers see the flags and the review detail. Parents never do. There is exactly one parent-facing surface, and it is deliberate: when your team submits a book order for the 30-day public comment period SB 13 requires, OnShelf publishes that list of titles to a public page. Parents see the list of titles under consideration. They do not see our flags, and they do not see your review notes.

Bookmarked informs. Districts decide.

We provide intelligence, not instructions. Data, not directives.

The one thing we most want you to know

We are here to help you keep books on shelves.

The worry we hear most is that a compliance tool means fewer books. Across our districts, the opposite is true. When a title needs action, it is reviewed and reclassified, not pulled, and the collection stays intact.

Reviewed and reclassified, not removed.
